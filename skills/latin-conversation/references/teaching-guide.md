# Conversation teaching guide

Status: original teaching draft. Examples have been checked while authoring, but have not been evaluated in an installed target AI tool or reviewed by an independent Latin teacher. They are constructed examples, not quotations.

## A small starting scene

Scene: two learners discussing reading. Do not invent biographical facts about the user.

| Latin | Meaning | Teaching point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salve! | Hello! (one person) | A singular greeting; `salvete` addresses more than one person. |
| Quid legis? | What are you reading? | `legis` is second-person singular present active indicative of `legere`. |
| Librum lego. | I am reading a book. | `librum` is accusative singular; `lego` supplies a first-person subject. |
| Quid amas? | What do you love/like? | `amas` is second-person singular; context determines the natural English rendering. |
| Linguam Latinam amo. | I love the Latin language. | `linguam` and `Latinam` agree in accusative singular feminine. |

The scene is for introductory pedagogical conversation. Follow a learner's chosen scenario when it can be handled accurately; a prepared scene is a fallback, not a mandatory lesson.

## Feedback example

Learner: `Ego amat linguam Latinam.`

Possible feedback in Chinese: “意思很清楚。这里主语是‘我’，动词用 `amo`，不是第三人称的 `amat`：`Ego amo linguam Latinam.` 也可以省略 `ego`，说 `Linguam Latinam amo.`”

Continue with a short relevant question only if the learner is practising a dialogue. Avoid adding vocabulary far above the demonstrated level.

## Hint ladder

If the learner asks for a hint, first name the issue (person, number, case or a missing word), then offer a form or small choice if still needed. Give the full answer when requested. A preference for hints does not justify indefinitely withholding an answer.

## Progress boundaries

Adapt to demonstrated performance in this conversation. “Beginner” is a teaching preference, not a validated diagnosis. Do not claim to hear pronunciation, remember a prior session, or sync the App's learning history.
